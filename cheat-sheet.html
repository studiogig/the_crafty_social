The Cheat Sheet

New to craft? Same. Here's everything we wish someone had told us when we started. No jargon, no judgement, just the basics.

· · · · · · ·

The Glossary

Every weird word explained. Honestly, we had to look most of these up too.

UK vs US terms: Crochet terms are different in the UK and US — a UK double crochet is a US single crochet. Confusing? Absolutely. Most YouTube tutorials use US terms. Most UK patterns use UK terms. Always check which one you're reading. We've flagged the differences below.

Crochet

Chain (ch)
The foundation of most crochet. Like building the first rung of a ladder. You'll start nearly every project with one.
Slip Stitch (sl st)
The shortest stitch. Used to join things together or move your yarn along without adding height. Think of it as a full stop.
Single Crochet (sc)
US term — in the UK this is called Double Crochet (dc)
The most basic stitch. Short, tight, and sturdy. Great for amigurumi (cute stuffed things) and anything that needs structure.
Double Crochet (dc)
US term — in the UK this is called Treble (tr)
Taller than single crochet. Creates a more open, flowing fabric. Blankets, scarves, granny squares — this is your go-to.
Half Double Crochet (hdc)
US term — UK: Half Treble (htr)
Right between a single and double crochet. The Goldilocks stitch — not too short, not too tall.
Treble Crochet (tr)
US term — UK: Double Treble (dtr)
A tall, open stitch. Great for lacy patterns and anything that needs to grow quickly.
Yarn Over (yo)
Wrapping the yarn around your hook. You'll do this approximately one million times per project. It becomes automatic, promise.
Magic Ring / Magic Circle
A way to start working in a circle with no hole in the middle. Looks like actual magic the first time you do it. YouTube it.
Turning Chain
The chain stitches you make at the start of a new row to get your yarn to the right height. Different stitches need different numbers.
Granny Square
The classic. A square motif usually made with double crochet clusters. Your nan probably made a blanket out of these. Now it's cool again.
Amigurumi
Japanese art of crocheting small stuffed creatures. Cute animals, food, characters — basically an excuse to make tiny things and put them on your desk.
Frogging
Pulling your work apart to start again. Called frogging because you "rip it, rip it" (ribbit). Honestly, it's quite satisfying.
Gauge / Tension
How tight or loose you crochet. Everyone's is different. Patterns ask you to check it so your finished thing is the right size. Important for clothes, less so for a wonky coaster.
WIP
Work In Progress. What you'll call the 47 half-finished projects on your sofa.
UFO
Unfinished Object. The WIPs you've given up on but can't bring yourself to frog. We all have them.
Stash
Your yarn collection. It will grow. It will take over a cupboard. You will claim you "needed" all of it.

Knitting

Cast On (CO)
Getting your first stitches onto the needle. There are about fifteen ways to do this. Start with long-tail cast on — it's the most useful.
Cast Off / Bind Off (BO)
Finishing your work by closing off the stitches so it doesn't unravel. The most satisfying moment in knitting.
Knit Stitch (K)
The most basic knitting stitch. Insert needle, wrap yarn, pull through. If you only learn one stitch, learn this one.
Purl Stitch (P)
The knit stitch's mirror image. Combine knit and purl and you can make almost anything. It's like learning a second chord on guitar.
Stockinette / Stocking Stitch
Alternating rows of knit and purl. Creates that classic smooth V-pattern you see on shop-bought jumpers.
Garter Stitch
Knit every row. Creates a bumpy, squishy fabric that lies flat. The easiest thing to knit — perfect for scarves.
DPNs (Double Pointed Needles)
Short needles with points at both ends. Used for knitting in the round — socks, hats, sleeves. They look terrifying but you'll get there.
Circular Needles
Two needles joined by a flexible cable. Used for knitting in the round or for big flat projects. Less stabby than DPNs.
Increase / Decrease
Adding or removing stitches to shape your work. How you turn a rectangle into a hat or a jumper sleeve.
Blocking
Wetting or steaming your finished piece and pinning it into shape. Makes everything look professionally finished. Game changer.
Tinking
"Knit" spelled backwards. Undoing your work one stitch at a time (as opposed to frogging, which is ripping the whole lot out).

Embroidery

Embroidery Hoop
The round frame that holds your fabric taut while you stitch. Comes in different sizes. Also looks cute as wall art when you're done.
Aida Cloth
A stiff, grid-like fabric made for cross stitch. The little holes make it easy to count and place your stitches evenly.
Embroidery Floss / Thread
Six-stranded thread. You can split it to use fewer strands for finer detail. DMC is the brand you'll see everywhere.
Backstitch
A basic outline stitch. Great for text, borders, and defining shapes. If you learn one embroidery stitch, make it this one.
Satin Stitch
Parallel stitches laid side by side to fill an area with solid colour. Looks beautiful. Takes patience. Worth it.
French Knot
A tiny, raised dot. The stitch that makes everyone swear at least once. Once you crack it, you'll put them on everything.
Cross Stitch
Two diagonal stitches that cross to form an X. Counted on a grid. Very meditative. Very good for rude words in pretty frames.
Chain Stitch
A series of loops that create a chain-like line. Great for outlines and filling. One of the oldest embroidery stitches in the world.
Lazy Daisy
A single chain stitch anchored at the end — looks like a petal. Group them in a circle and you've got a flower. Instant gratification.
Transfer
Getting your design onto the fabric. You can trace, use carbon paper, or buy iron-on transfers. No freehand drawing skills required.

Pattern Abbreviations

The shorthand you'll see in patterns. Print this page and stick it on your fridge.
